Nanakorobi Yaoki

Nanakorobi Yaoki (七転び八起き) is a Japanese Proverb that translates roughly to "Fall Down Seven Times, Stand Up 8." It is a message of perseverance, and resilence. It is a saying often associated with Daruma Dolls, dolls inspired by the appearance and teachings of the Buddhist monk Bodhidharma. This lesson is often physically represented though the traditional Japanese children's game, Daruma Otoshi, a game where you have to use a small wooden hammer to knock out wooden pegs without toppling the Daruma sitting on top.

The Significance Of 99

The number 99 is also one that holds an immense amount of significance in Japanese culture. It is one of the ages marked as a major milestone for within a person's life for being able to reach. 99 years is also often used as a generic way of refering to a "really long time," one example being its use within the name for household objects which have gained a soul after having existed for a very long time, known as tsukumogami. This process is believed to take 100 years, but the term "tsukumo" is itself thought to mean 'ninety-nine'.
It was these two ideas, which combined to form both the name, as well as the basic concepty for this game.
You may fall more than 7 times. It could take you over 99 attempts (okay, maybe that's a bit of an exaggeration). But every failure brings you closer to success. And through perseverance and resilience, you too can capture the monster found on the 7th floor, and win this game!
